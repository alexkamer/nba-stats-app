How Predictions Are Mademethod: v4-tuned-minutes-trend

A walkthrough of the player-prop & opponent-adjustment pipeline, as it works today — and what changed on Nov 16, 2026.

No ML model — this is a small, inspectable statistical blend of recent form, season average, minutes trend, opponent strength, and availability. Every projection is written once and never recomputed, so a pick can't quietly change after it's made.

1 Recent form + season average

Blend a player's recent games with their current-season average ▶

For the stat being projected (points, rebounds, a combo like points+rebounds, etc.), take the player's last 10 games and their current-season average, and blend:

blended = 0.6 × recentAverage(last 10 games) + 0.4 × seasonAverage(this season only) (only once there are ≥3 recent games — below that, season average alone)

This is computed from loadPlayerSeasonGameLog(playerId, seasonId) — scoped to one specific season. A missed game never has a box-score row, so it's automatically excluded from the recent-games window with no extra filtering.

2 Minutes trend

Dampen the projection if minutes are trending down ▶
trendMultiplier = avg(last 3 games' minutes) / avg(all recent games' minutes) clamped to [0.9×, 1.1×]

A player whose minutes have dropped recently gets scaled down; one blowout or garbage-time game can't swing it past the clamp. Tightened from an original [0.7×, 1.3×] after a hyperparameter sweep against 4 seasons of historical outcomes showed the tighter bound minimized prediction error for every tracked stat — see tune-minutes-trend-clamp (method: "v4-tuned-minutes-trend").

3 Opponent factor

Adjust for how much the specific opponent tends to allow in that stat ▶
opponentFactor = opponentTeam's allowed-average(this season only) / league average(this season only) clamped to [0.85×, 1.15×], neutral (1×) below 5 games of history

"What a team allows" is just what its opponents have scored against them, derived from already-ingested box scores — no new data source. Not computed for double-double/triple-double props, since those are achievement flags, not a stat a team "allows."

4 Teammate role-bump

Bump a player's projection when a teammate he shares minutes with is OUT ▶

Pool every game this season where any of tonight's absent teammates already sat out, and compare the player's average in those games to his own baseline projection. One combined multiplier — not one per absent teammate multiplied together (that compounds without bound when several teammates are out at once).

multiplier = avg(stat in games resembling tonight's absences) / baseline clamped to [0.85×, 1.3×], neutral (1×) below 2 qualifying games

5 Confidence & Best Bets

How a projection becomes a recommended pick ▶
edgeRatio = |projectedValue − marketLine| / stdev(recent games) bettable = edgeRatio ≥ 0.75 Best Bets = top 8 bettable picks, ranked by edgeRatio, one per player

stdev is the population standard deviation of the player's recent values for that stat — so a pick "beating the line by a lot relative to how volatile the player normally is" ranks above a pick beating the line by the same raw amount for a much steadier player.

What changed on Nov 16, 2026

Two of the inputs above — season average and opponent factor — were quietly pulling in data from every season on record (player box scores back to 2012, team box scores back to 2003) instead of just the current one. Both were found, measured with a backtest against real historical outcomes, and fixed the same day.

Bug 1 — season average

A veteran player's "season average" quietly included his entire career. Example: Andre Drummond's points+rebounds average came out to 22.66 (career, 1,008 games back to 2012) instead of 11.23 (his actual 13 games this season) — nearly double, from years as a high-minutes starter that no longer reflect his current bench role.

Bug 2 — opponent factor

A team's "how much do they allow" factor blended in 24 seasons of history. The league's scoring pace has risen roughly 10 points/game since 2003, so blending in that older, slower era systematically made every opponent factor skew too low. A backtest across 9,846 historical team-games showed this wasn't subtle:

Bias = average (predicted − actual). The old method wasn't just noisier, it was predicting too low on every single stat, every season, every stage of the season.

What's frozen, and what isn't

Every projection is tagged with a method string and never recomputed once written — the buggy version is v2-availability-aware, the season-scoping fix is v3-season-scoped, and the tightened minutes-trend clamp is v4-tuned-minutes-trend. All three coexist in history on purpose: it's what lets the accuracy page compare each era's track record honestly, instead of silently merging different methodologies into one number.
